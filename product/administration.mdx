Administration covers the screens where you configure an agent's credentials, adjust
platform-wide settings, and manage who belongs to your organization and what they can reach.
Some of these — **Secrets** and **Settings** — are available to everyone with a login. The rest
appear in the navigation only once your role includes organization or infrastructure management,
so what you see here depends on who you are. This page walks through each screen as it actually
appears; for the underlying role model, see [Access control overview](/onboarding/acl/overview).

## Secrets

The **Secrets** screen lists the credentials attached to an agent — API keys, database
connection strings, third-party tokens — as key/value pairs the agent reads from its container
environment.

Each row shows the secret's **Key** and when it was created. The **Value** column never displays
the actual secret — it always renders as a masked placeholder, both in the table and while typing
it into the form, and there's no way to reveal a previously-saved value from this screen. Secrets
are write-only from the UI's point of view: you can set them and overwrite them, but you can't
read one back.

To add a secret, click **Add Secret**, enter a **Key** (uppercase letters, numbers, and
underscores — `API_KEY` style) and a **Value**, then save. To remove one, click **Delete** next to
it and confirm.

<Note>
  Saving a secret here updates what's stored, encrypted, for the agent — it doesn't push the new
  value into an already-running container by itself. The new value takes effect the next time the
  agent is deployed or redeployed. See [Managing your agent's
  secrets](/platform/secret-manager/user-secrets) for how that applies across restarts and scaling events,
  and [Secret management overview](/platform/secret-manager/overview) for how encryption at rest works.
</Note>

## Settings

The **Settings** screen holds platform-wide configuration, organized into three tabs:

- **General** — the instance name shown in the UI, the default model used for chat, and the
  maximum output tokens allowed per request.
- **Models** — the Anthropic API key used for Claude model routing, and an optional OpenAI API
  key.
- **Registry** — the OCI registry URL, username, and password used for pulling agent images.

API keys and the registry password are entered through masked, password-style fields — like
secrets, they're stored encrypted and the screen doesn't show you a previously-saved value; you
only ever type in a new one. Changes on any tab apply once you click **Save Changes** at the
bottom of the screen.

## Organization and access screens

If your role includes managing people, agent access, or infrastructure, additional screens show
up alongside Secrets and Settings. Which ones you see — and what you can do on them — depends on
your role:

| Screen | Who it's for | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Access Control** | Organization administrators | Org-wide stats, health warnings, and a shortcut to place unassigned members into a department/team |
| **Users** | Organization administrators | Create, edit, deactivate, and delete user accounts; assign roles and placement |
| **Departments** | Team leads and above can view; org administrators manage | Browse departments and their rollups; create/edit is reserved for top-level administrators |
| **Teams** | Team leads and above can view; org administrators manage | Browse teams and their rollups; create/edit is reserved for top-level administrators |
| **Team Access** | Team leads, department managers, and administrators | Review and adjust the role and team placement of people in your own team or department |
| **Agent Runtime** | Administrators | View and manage the platform's underlying compute clusters |
| **Group Mappings** | Top-level administrators | Map identity-provider groups to Nasiko roles, teams, and departments |

The exact roles are `admin`, `department_manager`, `team_lead`, `team_member`, and `member` — see
[Access control overview](/onboarding/acl/overview) for the full hierarchy, and how the platform-wide
**superuser** flag (independent of role) unlocks a handful of these screens outright regardless of
role.

### Access Control

Access Control is a dashboard view of the whole organization: counts of admins, departments,
teams, users, and agents across the top, followed by warning cards for anything that needs
attention (for example, users with no team or department). Below that, a **Departments** table
rolls up each department's manager, team count, member count, and agent count, and an
**Unassigned Members** table lists users with no team or department yet.

From the unassigned members table, click the folder icon next to a user to open an **Assign
department / team** dialog — pick a department, optionally narrow to one of its teams, and save.
Leaving both fields empty clears the user's placement entirely.

If you land on this screen without the role to use it, it shows a plain error rather than any
data — there's no softened "not available" message here the way there is on Users.

### Users

Users lists every account on the platform, with columns for name/email, role, department/team,
active status, and last login. A filter bar lets you narrow the list by role, department, or
status, and the search box matches on name/username/email.

From here you can:

- **Add User** — enter a username, email, optional display name, and a role. There's no password
  to set: the platform generates a one-time **access key** and **access secret** on creation,
  shown once in the dialog immediately afterward (with copy buttons) — that access secret doubles
  as the user's login password, and the platform can't show it to you again after you close the
  dialog.
- **Edit** — update username, email, or display name, or set a new password (leave it blank to
  keep the current one).
- **Assign department/team** (the folder icon) — the same placement dialog described under Access
  Control.
- **Activate / Deactivate** — toggle whether the account can sign in.
- **Delete** — permanently remove the account.
- **Sync Azure AD Directory** — trigger a directory sync if your organization has one configured;
  a toast summarizes how many departments, teams, and users were created versus already present.

<Note>
  Users is reserved for organization administrators and above. If your role doesn't reach that
  level, the screen loads normally but tells you plainly that full user management "requires
  platform administrator access," and points you to Team Access instead for managing roles within
  your own team or department.
</Note>

### Departments and Teams

Departments and Teams are each a simple roster screen: a searchable table (name, manager/lead,
team or member counts, and agent count) plus stats at the top. Every team belongs to a department,
so creating one requires picking a department from a dropdown.

Anyone at team-lead level or above can browse these lists. Creating a new department or team, or
editing one that already exists, is reserved for the organization's top-level administrators —
everyone else sees the roster but not the create/edit controls taking effect.

### Team Access

Team Access is the scoped version of Users, for people who manage a team or department rather than
the whole organization. It shows only the users in your own team (or, for a department manager,
your whole department) alongside your role, with an **Edit Access** action per person to change
their role and — if you're an administrator or department manager — reassign their team.

You can only ever grant a role below your own, never at or above it — a team lead promoting
someone can hand them team member or team lead, never department manager or admin.

### Agent Runtime

Agent Runtime shows the compute clusters backing your deployed agents — provider (DigitalOcean,
AWS, Azure), region, node size, and status (from provisioning through ready to destroyed), with a
live provisioning log you can open per cluster and a **Destroy Cluster** action once it's no
longer transitional.

### Group Mappings

Group Mappings connects your identity provider's security groups to Nasiko roles. Each mapping
ties an external group's object ID to a role, a department, a team, or any combination — at least
one of the three has to be set. New logins through that identity provider are placed
automatically according to the highest-ranked matching mapping. The same **Sync Azure AD
Directory** action available on Users is here too, for pulling directory structure in directly
rather than waiting for people to log in.
